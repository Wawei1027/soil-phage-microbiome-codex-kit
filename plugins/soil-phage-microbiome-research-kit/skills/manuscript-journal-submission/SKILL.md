# Manuscript Journal Submission

Use this skill for manuscript planning, writing, revision, journal selection, cover letters, response to reviewers, submission checklists, and figure/table narrative checks.

## Non-negotiable research safety rules

- Do not fabricate results, citations, journal impact factors, journal quartiles, APCs, review times, editor names, species, databases, or software versions.
- For journal selection, verify current IF, JCR quartile, CiteScore, APC, article type, scope, and review time online or ask the user to provide current information.
- Do not overstate ecological interpretation beyond the design, sample size, statistics, and data quality.
- Do not claim that an analysis was performed unless it is present in the manuscript, scripts, or supplied results.

## Manuscript structure

Check and help draft:

- Title.
- Abstract.
- Introduction.
- Methods.
- Results.
- Discussion.
- Conclusion.

For microbiome, metagenomics, phage, and soil ecology manuscripts, focus on whether:

- The research question is clear.
- The sample size and design support the claims.
- Metadata and environmental variables are sufficient.
- Methods are reproducible.
- Software versions, database paths, and database dates are reported where available.
- Statistical methods match the data type and design.
- Figures and tables tell a coherent story.
- Ecological interpretation is evidence-based and not overstated.

## Journal selection workflow

When comparing journals, evaluate:

- Scope.
- Article type.
- Open access fee or APC.
- Impact factor, JCR quartile, CiteScore, or other metrics if verified.
- Review time if verified.
- Target readers.
- Whether the journal regularly publishes microbial ecology, metagenomics, phage, virome, or soil ecology papers.
- Data and code policy.
- Figure, table, word count, and supplementary material limits.

Never invent journal metrics. If browsing is unavailable or current information is missing, ask the user for journal pages or mark the item as "needs verification".

## Revision workflow

When editing a manuscript, use three layers of feedback:

- Major scientific issue: study design, data support, statistical validity, reproducibility, missing controls, unsupported interpretation.
- Structure and logic issue: flow among paragraphs, result order, figure narrative, section balance, claim placement.
- Sentence-level issue: clarity, concision, grammar, terminology, and journal style.

## Response to reviewers workflow

- Stay polite and specific.
- Respond point by point.
- Quote or summarize the reviewer comment clearly.
- State what was changed and where.
- Distinguish completed analyses from analyses that were not performed.
- Do not admit to analyses, data, controls, or citations that do not exist.
- If disagreeing, explain the scientific reason and offer a manuscript clarification when appropriate.

## Expected outputs

When triggered, produce:

- A manuscript diagnosis or writing plan.
- Section-by-section edits or draft text.
- A journal comparison table with unverified fields clearly marked.
- A cover letter draft when requested.
- A point-by-point response to reviewers using the supplied reviewer comments.
- A submission checklist covering files, metadata, data availability, code availability, ethics or permits if relevant, figures, tables, and supplementary materials.
